1. Decoding of the targets from the preparatory state is trivial. The agent has access to the targets in the horizon. So, their location is obviously decodable. However, the future movement  parameters, such as peak velocity, segment duration, decceleration/acceleration times and other kinematic measures are not trivially represented. The question is, when they become decodable in the output-null subspace. Is it during the planning period such that everything is decided? If so, does it depend on the planning duration available? Interestingly, the T2 jump reflects that the agent is online planning. So how much of the decoding of these kinematics information carries on to during the movement is also an interesting question. 

2. Reaction time is very fast. Possibly due to no sensory delay in the paradigm. The ET floor is 20ms since the simulation step is 20ms. The agent is also producing very fast RTs ranging from 28ms to 20ms depending on the duration of the planning available. The curve looks quite similar to monkeys and it is an emergent behaviour as we did not intend anything about the reaction time. So it is important to investigate this. Is this trivial or does it have an underlying reason? Why does short planning duration (250ms) result in a slower reaction time than long planning duration? What is this observation? Is this related to the preplanning requried for the reaching? 

3. The T1 jump inwards test is not really optimal here as it just jumps the T1 onto the point mass. A better testing need to happen for T1 jump. Will different extents of jump result in different kinematics for reaching to T1? When does the kinematics start diverging compared to when there is no jump at all. What computations occur in the agent? Can we find an online-planning subspace? Is it the same as the output-null subspace identified earlier? Furthermore, how is the jump behaviour different when we have a T2 or we do not have a T2. One jump possibility is jumping inwards a bit but still to the direction that the point mass is moving to. The other jump is jumping behind the point mass which requries a decelleration. Does the agent behave similarly across the two? 

4. The T1 jump outwards results in a timeout. What if we give more time to the agent? How will it adjust its behaviour? This needs to be tested better.

5. T3 is sometimes given as the horizon in this task. However, the dependency of the reach on T3 is not discussed and tested. Theoretically, would an optimised policy need to account for T3? What about the agent? Does it care at all about the third target? 

6. More targets. What is the planning capacity? Does the agent only track the second target or could account for multiple? This is the expansion of point "5" so might depend on what we see there.

7. We can do a churchland-like and Armin-like experiment in the 1D. For Churchland like experiment, we will place the first target close to the starting point. Then the second target is placed in different locations. In the preview two, case, the closeness of the T1 ensures that the kinematics are roughly the same. This allows us to look at whether the planning stage the output-null subspace is planning for the T2 trajectory or not. For example whether it needs higher velocity or not. What if T2 is very far from T1? What if it is very close? What if it is very close but to the inside of it such that the agent has to deccelerate?
